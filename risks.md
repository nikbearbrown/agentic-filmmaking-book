## Comparable Texts Analysis

**1. Blain Brown, *The Basics of Filmmaking: Screenwriting, Producing, Directing, Cinematography, Audio, Editing* (Routledge, 2020)**
- *Reader:* introductory film production students.
- *Strongest:* department-by-department coverage of a real production, with forms, terminology and safety.
- *Weakest:* no AI; assumes a crew, a set and a budget.
- *Why a learner might choose it:* an established, thorough reference.
- *Why choose this course:* the same departments, as vocabulary for one person directing an agent.

**2. Tom Barrance, *Start Making Movies* (Learnaboutfilm, 2021)**
- *Reader:* beginners and schools.
- *Strongest:* a plain step-by-step route: story, picture, sound, sequence, time, essential scenes, editing and sharing.
- *Weakest:* no AI; the depth stops at the beginner level.
- *Why choose it:* short and approachable.
- *Why choose this course:* the same beginner route, carried through AI generation to a released opening scene.

**3. Kelly Gordon Brine, *The Art of Cinematic Storytelling* (Oxford University Press, 2020)**
- *Reader:* directing and storyboarding students.
- *Strongest:* the deepest shot and cut grammar in the pantry, with ready-made recipes for dialogue, chases, crowds and driving scenes.
- *Weakest:* no producing, no sound, no AI.
- *Why choose it:* for shot design alone.
- *Why choose this course:* it uses Brine's grammar inside a whole pipeline, from script to release.

**4. Curious Refuge, *AI Filmmaking Course* (online, four weeks; syllabus in the pantry, undated)**
- *Reader:* creatives who want AI tools quickly.
- *Strongest:* broad tool coverage with a project every week.
- *Weakest:* little craft; tied to specific tools; its week 2 goals include removing watermarks from videos.
- *Why choose it:* short and practical.
- *Why choose this course:* craft that outlasts the tools, an agent the learner directs, honest disclosure, and no creative calls made for them.

**5. James Hutson and Andrew Allen Smith, *Cinematic Algorithms: The Rise of Generative AI in Video Art and Visual Culture* (CRC Press, 2025, 1st edition)**
- *Reader:* students and scholars of media, art and AI.
- *Strongest:* history, ethics and authorship, and 12 interviews with AI filmmakers.
- *Weakest:* a tool chapter that dated within a year; no exercises; no cost.
- *Why choose it:* to study AI and visual culture.
- *Why choose this course:* to make a film.

## Differentiation Statements

- Unlike *The Basics of Filmmaking*, which teaches a crewed production by department, this course teaches the same craft as the vocabulary for directing an agent, for learners with a vision and no crew.
- Unlike *Start Making Movies*, which stops at phone filmmaking, this course carries beginners through AI generation to release, for learners who are lost on the tech.
- Unlike *The Art of Cinematic Storytelling*, which teaches shot design in depth, this course puts shot design inside the whole pipeline, for learners who want to finish a film.
- Unlike the Curious Refuge course, which teaches tools week by week, this course teaches filmmaking first with tools in dated boxes, for learners who want skills that survive the next model release.
- Unlike *Cinematic Algorithms*, which surveys AI in film, this course produces a film with every cost and failure on the record, for learners who want to make work, not study it.

## Adoption Risk Register

**Field stability** (Content)
- *Likelihood:* High; the pantry's 2025 tool chapter already names tools Bear's 2026 runs did not use.
- *Impact:* High.
- *Trigger:* a named model or price in a chapter is wrong at publication.
- *Mitigation:* tools and prices only in dated boxes; defaults, overrides and judgment in the running text.
- *Contingency:* refresh the boxes each edition.

**Learner cost** (Market)
- *Likelihood:* High. *Impact:* High.
- *Trigger:* learners cannot finish Part III for lack of credits.
- *Mitigation:* the cost order and the two still tiers (most exercises run on draft stills); a test budget in Ch 9; a free or low-cost path.
- *Contingency:* a stills-only route through the course, with motion optional. [NEEDS HUMAN INPUT — who pays]

**Self-paced drop-off** (Structure)
- *Likelihood:* Medium to High. *Impact:* High.
- *Trigger:* learners stop at the gate between stills and motion (Ch 9), where the spending starts.
- *Mitigation:* the pebble in the Introduction gives an early finished film; every chapter ends in a file; the capstone is the learner's own scene.
- *Contingency:* make the capstone possible as a reviewed storyboard with only a few clips.

**Relay creep** (Content)
- *Likelihood:* Medium. *Impact:* High.
- *Trigger:* Bear, or the course, starts relaying "change this, update that" or making a filmmaker's creative calls.
- *Mitigation:* every "On the set" step not yet run is a session Galen runs himself; templates with blanks; Galen's prompts shown only as his decisions.
- *Contingency:* pull the chapter back to "flag once, the filmmaker decides, log it".

## Top 3 Adoption Risks

**1. Learners cannot afford the expensive steps.** Motion is where the money goes, and the learner this course is written for is the one without a budget. The cost order and the two still tiers help, because most of the work happens on cheap draft stills. But the course still needs a decided answer to who pays and a stills-first route, or it excludes its own audience.

**2. The AI half ages faster than the course ships.** Every AI source in the pantry was out of step with Bear's own tools within a year. If tool names and prices leak into the running text, the course looks stale on day one. The anatomy keeps them in dated boxes. The discipline is holding that line in every chapter.

**3. Consent, rights, and the creative calls.** The running case is a real person's story and characters. The exercises involve likeness, voices and music. The thesis promises that the course never makes the creative calls. One withdrawn consent, one rights dispute, or one chapter that decides for the filmmaker would damage the course's central claim. The defenses are written consent from Galen, rights records in every exercise, and sessions Galen runs himself.
